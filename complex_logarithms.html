Logarithm Function extended to Negative Numbers

What is the logarithm of a negative number?

$$ ln(-1) = \text{ }? $$

Let's derive it!

-1 can be written as: $$ -1 = i^{2}$$ $$ ln(i^{2}) = 2*ln(i) \text{ via logarithmic property rule: } ln(a^{b}) = b*ln(a) $$

Let's refer to euler's formula $$ e^{i\theta} = cos(\theta) + i*sin(\theta) $$ $$ \text{We want to seperate i, so } \theta = \frac{\pi}{2} $$ $$ e^{\frac{\pi}{2} i } = cos(\frac{\pi}{2}) + i*sin(\frac{\pi}{2}) = i $$ $$ e^{\frac{\pi}{2} i } = i $$ We can substitute this result back into our logarithmic equation $$ 2*ln(i) = 2*ln(e^{\frac{\pi}{2} i }) = 2 * \frac{\pi}{2} i \text{ via logarithmic property: } ln(e^{b}) = b $$ $$ 2*ln(i) = \pi i $$ Therefore: $$ ln(-1) = \pi i $$

Now for the generalization:

Here is another logarithmic rule: $$ \log_b(a) = \frac{\log_c(a)}{\log_c(b)}$$ For the purposes of simplicity, let: $$ c = e$$ $$ \log_b(a) = \frac{\ln(a)}{\ln(b)} $$ Now, let's approach our problem: $$ \log_b(-a) = \frac{\ln(-a)}{\ln(b)} $$ The numerator can be rewritten as: $$ ln(-a) = ln(-1) + ln(a) \text{ via product rule: } ln(ab) = ln(a) + ln(b) $$ We already know what ln(-1) is: $$ \log_b(-a) = \frac{\ln(-a)}{\ln(b)} = \frac{ln(-1) + ln(a)}{\ln(b)} = \frac{\pi i + ln(a)}{\ln(b)} = \log_b(a) + \frac{\pi i}{ln(b)}$$ Finally: $$ \log_b(-a) = \log_b(a) + \frac{\pi i}{ln(b)} \quad a, b \in \mathbb{R} \quad a,b > 0 $$
