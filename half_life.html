Integrated Rate Laws

At the half-life, the concentration will be half of what it started with, so: $$ [A] = \frac{1}{2}[A_0] $$

Using this let's derive the half life for a first order reaction.

$$ln[A] - ln[A_0] = -kt $$

$$ln\frac{[A]}{[A_0]} = -kt $$

$$ln\frac{\frac{[A_0]}{2}}{[A_0]} = -kt_{\frac{1}{2}} $$

$$ln\frac{1}{2} = -kt_{\frac{1}{2}} $$

$$-ln(2) = -kt_{\frac{1}{2}} $$

$$ln(2) = kt_{\frac{1}{2}} $$

$$t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{ln(2)}{k} $$

This shows us that the half life of a FIRST order reaction is not dependant on the rate constant.

Using the same substitution process, let's derive the half-life formula for a 0th order reaction.

$$[A] - [A_0] = -kt $$

$$\frac{1}{2}[A_0] - [A_0] = -kt_{\frac{1}{2}} $$

$$[A_0]-\frac{1}{2}[A_0] = kt_{\frac{1}{2}} $$

$$ kt_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}[A_0]$$

$$ t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{[A_0]}{2k}$$

Order Number Integrated Rate Law Substituting $$[A] = \frac{1}{2} [A_0]$$ Half-Life Formula
$n=0$ $[A] = -kt + [A_0] $ $\frac{1}{2} [A_0] = -kt_{\frac{1}{2}} + [A_0] $ $$ t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{[A_0]}{2k}$$
$n=1$ $ln[A] = -kt+ln[A_0]$ $\frac{1}{2} [A_0] = [A_0] e^{-kt_{\frac{1}{2}}}$ $$t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{ln(2)}{k} $$
$n=2$ $\frac{1}{[A]} = kt+\frac{1}{[A_0]}$ $\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2} [A_0]} = kt_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{[A_0]}$ $$t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{[A_0]k} $$
$n=3$ $\frac{1}{2[A]^2} = kt+\frac{1}{2[A_0]^2}$ $\frac{1}{2(\frac{1}{2} [A_0])^2} = kt_{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2[A_0]^2}$ $$t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{3}{2[A_0]^2k} $$
$n \neq 1$ $$\frac{[A]^{-n+1}}{-n+1} - \frac{[A_0]^{-n+1}}{-n+1} = -kt$$ $$\frac{(\frac{1}{2} [A_0])^{-n+1}}{-n+1} - \frac{[A_0]^{-n+1}}{-n+1} = -kt_{\frac{1}{2}}$$ $$t_{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{2^{n-1} - 1}{k(n-1)([A_0]^{1-n})} $$
