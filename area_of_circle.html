Area of a Circle

The formula of the area of a circle is:

$$ A = \pi R^2 $$

Let's derive it!

1) Defining the Circle Function

The function of a circle is given by $$x^2 + y^2 = R^2$$

This function can be even further split up to solve for its y-counterparts.

$$x^2 + y^2 = R^2$$

$$y^2 = R^2 - x^2$$

$$y = \pm \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$$

$$y = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2}$$ represents the top half of the circle.

$$\begin{aligned} y = -\sqrt{R^2 - x^2} \end{aligned} $$ represents the bottom half of the circle.

To find the area of this circle, we can use this formula:

$$ \int_{x_1}^{x_2} (f(x)-g(x))\,dx $$

This formula gives you the area between two functions. Where f(x) is the top function & g(x) is the bottom function.

$$\begin{aligned} f(x) = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2} \end{aligned} $$
$$\begin{aligned} g(x) = -\sqrt{R^2 - x^2} \end{aligned} $$
$$\begin{aligned} f(x)-g(x) = \sqrt{R^2 - x^2} - (-\sqrt{R^2 - x^2}) = 2\sqrt{R^2 - x^2} \end{aligned} $$
$$\begin{aligned} f(x)=g(x), x = -R, R \end{aligned} $$
$$\begin{aligned} x_1 = -R \end{aligned} $$ $$\begin{aligned} x_2 = R \end{aligned} $$

$$ \int_{-R}^{R} (2\sqrt{R^2 - x^2})\,dx = 2\int_{-R}^{R} (\sqrt{R^2 - x^2})\,dx $$

$$ 2\int_{-R}^{R} (\sqrt{R^2 - x^2})\,dx = 2\int_{-R}^{R} \sqrt{R^2(1 - \frac{x^2}{R^2})}\,dx $$

$$ 2\int_{-R}^{R} \sqrt{R^2(1 - \frac{x^2}{R^2})}\,dx = 2\sqrt{R^2}\int_{-R}^{R} \sqrt{1 - \frac{x^2}{R^2}}\,dx = 2|R|\int_{-R}^{R} \sqrt{1 - (\frac{x}{R})^2}\,dx$$

$$ sin(\theta) = \frac{x}{R} $$

$$ x = R*sin(\theta) $$

$$ dx = R*cos(\theta)d\theta $$

$$ sin(\theta) = \frac{x}{R} $$

$$ \theta = arcsin(\frac{x}{R}) $$

$$ \theta_1 = arcsin(\frac{x_1}{R}) = arcsin(\frac{-R}{R}) = arcsin(-1) = -\frac{\pi}{2} $$

$$ \theta_1 = -\frac{\pi}{2} $$

$$ \theta_2 = arcsin(\frac{x_2}{R}) = arcsin(\frac{R}{R}) = arcsin(1) = \frac{\pi}{2} $$

$$ \theta_2= \frac{\pi}{2} $$

$$ 2R\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{1 - (\frac{R*sin(\theta)}{R})^2}\,Rcos(\theta)d\theta = 2R^2\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{1 - sin^2(\theta)}\,cos(\theta)d\theta$$

$$ sin^2(\theta) + cos^2(\theta) = 1$$

$$ cos^2(\theta) = 1 - sin^2(\theta)$$

$$ 2R^2\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \sqrt{cos^2(\theta)}\,cos(\theta)d\theta = 2R^2\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} cos^2(\theta)d\theta $$

$$ cos^2(\theta) - sin^2(\theta) = cos(2\theta)$$

$$ sin^2(\theta) = 1 - cos^2(\theta)$$

$$ cos(2\theta) = cos^2(\theta) - (1 - cos^2(\theta)) = cos^2(\theta) - 1 + cos^2(\theta) = 2cos^2(\theta) - 1 $$

$$ cos(2\theta) = 2cos^2(\theta) - 1 $$

$$ cos(2\theta) + 1 = 2cos^2(\theta) $$

$$ cos^2(\theta) = \frac{cos(2\theta) + 1}{2} $$

$$ 2R^2\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} cos^2(\theta)d\theta = 2R^2\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} \frac{cos(2\theta) + 1}{2}d\theta = R^2\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} (cos(2\theta) + 1)d\theta $$

$$ R^2 \int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}( cos(2\theta) + 1 )d\theta = R^2 \left[ \frac{sin(2\theta)}{2} + \theta \right]_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}$$

$$ R^2 \left[ \frac{sin(2\theta)}{2} + \theta \right]_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}} = R^2((\frac{sin(2(\frac{\pi}{2}))}{2} + \frac{\pi}{2})-(\frac{sin(2(-\frac{\pi}{2}))}{2} -\frac{\pi}{2})) = R^2((0 + \frac{\pi}{2})-(0 -\frac{\pi}{2})) = R^2(\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\pi}{2}) = \pi R^2 $$

$$ Area = \pi R^2 $$
